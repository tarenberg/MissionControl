# Investment Research: Atoco
**Date:** Friday, May 15, 2026
**Topic:** Carbon Capture and Atmospheric Water Harvesting (AWH)

## Overview
Atoco is a deep-tech company focused on atmospheric chemistry and molecular engineering to solve two of the most pressing global challenges: water scarcity and carbon emissions.

## The Science: Reticular Chemistry
The company was founded by **Professor Omar Yaghi** (UC Berkeley). Yaghi is widely considered the pioneer of **Reticular Chemistry**, specifically the invention of **Metal-Organic Frameworks (MOFs)** and **Covalent Organic Frameworks (COFs)**.
- **MOFs** are porous crystalline materials that can be designed with atomic precision.
- They have record-breaking surface areas (one gram can have the surface area of a football field), allowing them to trap specific molecules like H2O or CO2 from the air.

## Key Technologies
1. **Atmospheric Water Harvesting (AWH):**
   - Unlike traditional desalination or condensation (which require high humidity), Atoco's MOF-based systems can extract pure water from the air even in **arid, low-humidity environments** (as low as 5-10% humidity).
   - The process is powered by ambient heat or low-energy sources, making it sustainable for remote regions.

2. **Carbon Capture (DAC):**
   - Atoco develops solid-state modules for **Direct Air Capture (DAC)**.
   - Their materials are designed to selectively bind CO2 while ignoring other atmospheric gases, with higher efficiency and lower energy requirements than first-generation liquid-amine systems.

## Investment Thesis
- **Strong IP/Moat:** Built on decades of foundational research by a world-leading scientist (Yaghi is often cited as a Nobel Prize candidate).
- **Dual-Market Addressability:** Solves for both climate adaptation (water) and climate mitigation (carbon).
- **Scalability:** The materials are modular and can be integrated into various systems from small-scale household water harvesters to industrial-scale carbon capture plants.
- **Strategic Timing:** Global mandates for carbon removal and the worsening global water crisis create a strong tailwind for Atoco's technology.

## Risks
- **Manufacturing Scale-Up:** Transitioning from lab-scale MOF synthesis to mass production of tons of material while maintaining crystalline quality.
- **Cost Competitiveness:** MOFs can be expensive to produce; the company must demonstrate a clear path to cost parity with alternative water/carbon solutions.
- **Competitive Landscape:** Other DAC players (Climeworks, Carbon Engineering) are further along in deployment, though Atoco's material science might be superior.

## Conclusion
Atoco represents a high-conviction bet on the "materials revolution" in climate tech. Its success depends on the transition from a research-heavy entity to a robust industrial manufacturer.
